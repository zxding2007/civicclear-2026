# CivicClear | Municipal Readiness Report
**CIVIC Hack 2026 · Toronto pilot · 12 November 2026 · one-page equivalent**

**Use and value.** CivicClear is for residents who need to tell the difference between staff recommendations, committee steps and final City Council decisions. Potential municipal users include the Clerk's Office, communications, engagement teams and councillors' staff. A resident chooses an official Toronto agenda item and optionally a ward, sees a source-linked plain-language card and finds the proper participation link. This can reduce the false impression that “received for information” equals approval.

**Data, operations and resources.** Inputs are public TMMIS agenda records, official report PDFs and authoritative tax-rate tables. The prototype provides four curated agenda examples and tests without API access. Optional on-demand analysis uses Python/Streamlit, a Claude API account and a password-protected analyst page. Before publishing AI-generated cards, an assigned municipal reviewer should inspect the *full* official record, verify status, dates, affected wards, amendments, citations and current participation notices. A prototype deployment can use Streamlit Community Cloud; production should use city-approved access, hosting and logging.

**Rough cost and capacity.** Public cached examples incur no LLM inference charges when viewed. At the reviewed Claude Opus 5.5 list prices of USD $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, a two-call item review is expected to cost in the order of cents to dollars depending on PDF size; this is an *estimate*, not a measured bill. Budget separately for operator validation, updates, accessible design, security review, backups and hosting. The paid API path has not been exercised in this build.

**Privacy and governance.** Public records can still contain private correspondents' names or contact details. The initial tool removes `Communications` and `Speakers` sections from pasted pages before an LLM request, and does not ask residents for names or addresses. **This does not scrub uploaded PDFs or guarantee full anonymization.** A real city pilot requires privacy assessment, human PDF review, security controls, third-party data-processing approval, retention policy and accessible alternatives.

**What residents must know.** Explanations may omit context or be wrong; the City of Toronto's original item and meeting notices always control. A committee decision is not automatically a Council decision. A request for future consultation does not mean comments are being accepted today. If the system cannot confirm a deadline, it says so. The ward field is a coarse location clue, not a complete property-by-property impact analysis. The tax calculator is illustrative, holds assessment constant and excludes education tax.

**Adaptation and inclusion.** Other municipalities can substitute their official agenda sources, terminology glossary, procedural mapping and applicable public-participation rules. First validate the status logic and citations against a representative local corpus, then test against real resident tasks and municipal clerk review. The next iteration should provide multilingual support, WCAG-informed keyboard/screen-reader checks, printable summaries and offline distribution for residents without dependable internet or English fluency.
