# CivicClear — Municipal Readiness Report
**CIVIC Hack 2026 · City of Toronto pilot · November 12, 2026 · one-page submission companion**

**Who uses it and why.** Residents can browse real municipal agenda items, learn what was *proposed* versus *adopted*, identify wards named in the decision, and follow source-linked participation instructions. Toronto Clerk's Office, public engagement and communications teams are possible municipal stewards; no City endorsement is claimed. CivicClear correctly distinguishes “received for information” from a project approval.

**Data, operations and resources.** The demo reads public Toronto Meeting Management Information System (TMMIS) items, project updates, publicly advertised feedback channels and adopted residential municipal tax rates. Four curated cases are cached in GitHub and displayed through Python/Streamlit on Render (free web service); opening them makes no paid model requests. A protected, optional Claude extraction/citation pipeline is coded but **not live-API tested**. Municipal adoption would require a named reviewer, complete source/PDF review, update monitoring and deployment controls.

**Rough cost and feasibility.** The current Render deployment is on its free plan (subject to provider limits), and cached views cost $0 in LLM inference. Any future Claude use is metered by input/output volume and PDF size, and should have a documented budget cap. The larger ongoing municipal costs will be staff checking, monitoring changed decisions and deadlines, accessibility support, approved hosting, security and procurement. No measured Claude bill is claimed.

**Privacy and governance.** Resident browsing requires no name or street address. Curated public excerpts avoid copying contributor correspondence. Initial redaction removes named Communications/Speakers sections from pasted pages, but **not** sensitive information inside uploaded PDFs. A city pilot needs privacy impact assessment, review of third-party processing/retention terms, threat assessment, access control, accessibility testing, and manual checks before external AI processing.

**What residents must know.** This is informational, not legal, planning or tax advice; official records govern. Quotes may be correct yet omit context. The Little Jamaica committee action is not final City Council adoption, while a *separate* City project portal advertises a specific interactive feedback map through Nov 30, 2026. Dates can change and need re-checking. Stakeholder impacts are discussion prompts, **not quantified predictions**. The municipal tax comparison holds assessed value constant and excludes education tax.

**Adaptation and inclusion.** Other cities can replace Toronto agenda parsers, procedure vocabulary, public consultation links and tax tables, then validate on clerk-reviewed local cases. Next pilot tests: keyboard/screen-reader and mobile accessibility, plain-language/multilingual access, community user testing, and a documented human source sign-off. **Independent municipal/team approval of all demo claims remains pending.**
